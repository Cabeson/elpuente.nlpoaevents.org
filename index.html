Welcome

One week, one association, two hundred and sixty-one pictures.

This is the story of El Puente, the 2026 NLPOA national conference and convention, told the way the people who were there will remember it: in photographs.

It follows the week in order, from a send-off in New Orleans to the last group photo at the awards gala in Omaha. Each chapter opens with a short story about the place or event, followed by every photo taken there.

Chapter 1 of 14 · Sunday, October 11

The send-off

New Orleans, Louisiana

The trip began in New Orleans, Louisiana, the city on the Mississippi River known for its food, its music and the French Quarter.

Before heading north, a little of that Louisiana flavor was packed for the road: two shakers of Cajun seasoning, ready for a week on the Great Plains.

From here the trip ran north to Omaha, Nebraska, home of the 2026 national conference and convention.

Chapter 2 of 14 · Monday, October 12

Arrival in Omaha

Downtown Omaha

Omaha sits on the Missouri River in eastern Nebraska. Its downtown mixes old brick warehouse buildings with new towers of glass and steel.

On arrival day the sky was clear and blue. The first photos look up at a tall glass tower and the Brickline sign on a downtown corner.

After a long day of travel, the hotel lounge was the place to rest before the week began.

Chapter 4 of 14 · Tuesday, October 13 · Afternoon

A Maverick welcome

University of Nebraska Omaha

The University of Nebraska Omaha is home of the Mavericks. On Tuesday afternoon the group toured its campus.

The tour started by a University of Nebraska police vehicle and the Mavericks mascot statue. It continued through the athletic facilities: a fieldhouse, a competition pool, and a ballpark with seats under a white shade canopy.

Near the ballpark, a sculpture honors Omaha’s long history as home of the College World Series. The last stop was the university’s hockey arena.

Chapter 7 of 14 · Wednesday, October 14 (date estimated)

A visit to Boys Town

Boys Town, Nebraska

Boys Town is a village just west of Omaha. Father Edward Flanagan founded it in 1917 as a home for boys who had nowhere else to go, and it still cares for children and families today.

The group visited its historic grounds, including a brick house with a statue out front, and the museum, which tells the village’s story.

Inside the museum, a vintage Boys Town bus is on display, and the group stopped there for photos.

Chapter 9 of 14 · Friday, October 16 · Sunrise

Memorial at dawn

Nebraska Vietnam Veterans Memorial

The Nebraska Vietnam Veterans Memorial honors the Nebraskans who served in the Vietnam War. Early Friday, a group visited at first light.

A long wall carries the memorial’s name and the emblems of the armed services. A Huey helicopter is mounted high above the grounds. The American flag and the POW/MIA flag fly overhead, beside the Hall of Honor.

Stone markers carry the seals of each military branch. The group walked the grounds and gathered for photos as the day began.

Chapter 14 of 14 · Saturday, October 17 · Evening

The awards gala

The conference hotel

The week ended on Saturday night with the 2026 Formal Awards Gala in the hotel ballroom.

The ballroom was set with round tables and chandeliers. Honorees received awards and certificates, then posed at the photo wall with family, friends and their chapters.

A mariachi band played between courses, and the night ended on the dance floor.

It was the final night of the week in Omaha.

Officer of the Year

Steven DeJesus

Officer, New York City Police Department, NY

NLPOA Bronx Chapter

Member since 2023

14years with the NYPD
Mar 72026, Gracie Mansion
0devices detonated

“Courage under fire.”

Steven DeJesus is a 14-year veteran of the New York City Police Department and a member of the NLPOA Bronx Chapter.

On March 7, 2026, an ISIS-inspired attempted bombing took place outside Gracie Mansion, the official residence of the Mayor of New York City, during a clash between a demonstration and a counterprotest. Two suspects, 18 and 19, had traveled from Pennsylvania and allegedly threw two homemade explosive devices, one of them containing the highly volatile explosive TATP.

Not knowing exactly what was being thrown, Officer DeJesus and Detective Miguel Rivera tackled and arrested one suspect while other officers tackled the second. Neither device detonated. Federal prosecutors later indicted both men on eight charges.

★ ★ ★Selected as the 2026 NLPOA Officer of the Year for his bravery and commitment to law enforcement excellence.

“The same cuchifrito on Myrtle and Knickerbocker is the one you’ll find here on 104th and 3rd Avenue, in the heart of El Barrio.”

Miguel Rivera is Vice President of the NLPOA Brooklyn Chapter. On March 7, 2026, during the ISIS-inspired attempted bombing outside Gracie Mansion, he and Officer Steven DeJesus tackled and arrested one of the suspects as an improvised explosive device was being thrown.

Of Puerto Rican descent, Rivera was born in 1983 at Manhattan Hospital and raised in Bushwick, Brooklyn. He began his career in 2006 in the 23rd Precinct, Spanish Harlem, with eight years on patrol and three in the Domestic Violence office. As a Neighborhood Coordination Officer he worked closely with the communities of El Barrio, and was promoted to Detective in June 2021. Today he is a Community Affairs Officer.

Meritorious Award

Christian Robinson

Deputy, Dallas County Sheriff’s Office, TX

NLPOA Dallas County Chapter

Member since 2022

Oct 272025
3:00 a.m.on an I-35 overpass
Surviveda fall over the bridge railing

On October 27, 2025, at around 3:00 a.m., Deputy Christian Robinson of the Dallas County Sheriff’s Department was outside his patrol vehicle on an I-35 overpass near Harry Hines Boulevard, investigating an earlier crash on the slick roadway.

A pickup truck hydroplaned and slammed into his patrol vehicle, knocking him over the bridge railing and onto the embankment below.

Despite serious injuries, Deputy Robinson stayed conscious and was able to radio for help. He was taken to Parkland Hospital. A detainee in his SUV was evaluated at the hospital and was uninjured, as was the driver of the pickup.

★ ★ ★Selected by NLPOA National for the Meritorious Award for his outstanding commitment to serve, regardless of the danger involved.

Meritorious Award

Michael Valladarez

Sergeant, Dallas County Sheriff’s Office, TX

NLPOA Dallas County Chapter President

Member since 2010

30minutes of lifesaving CPR
No AEDavailable
3rescuers working together

On May 12, 2026, Sgt. Michael Valladarez was traveling to Orlando, Florida. At the airport he saw an older woman in medical distress, her face blue and her chest not moving.

With no AED defibrillator available, he began lifesaving CPR. An off-duty officer from Billings, Montana, whom he had never met, stepped in to help, and Austin Chadwell of the U.S. Army joined the rescue.

Together they kept up life-saving techniques for 30 minutes until the ambulance arrived, and the woman was taken to the hospital emergency room.

★ ★ ★The NLPOA National Meritorious Award recognizes Michael Valladarez for his outstanding selfless act.

Chapter 7 of 14 · Wednesday, October 14 (date estimated)

A visit to Boys Town

Boys Town, Nebraska

Boys Town is a village just west of Omaha. Father Edward Flanagan founded it in 1917 as a home for boys who had nowhere else to go, and it still cares for children and families today.

The group visited its historic grounds, including a brick house with a statue out front, and the museum, which tells the village’s story.

Inside the museum, a vintage Boys Town bus is on display, and the group stopped there for photos.

Chapter 9 of 14 · Friday, October 16 · Sunrise

Memorial at dawn

Nebraska Vietnam Veterans Memorial

The Nebraska Vietnam Veterans Memorial honors the Nebraskans who served in the Vietnam War. Early Friday, a group visited at first light.

A long wall carries the memorial’s name and the emblems of the armed services. A Huey helicopter is mounted high above the grounds. The American flag and the POW/MIA flag fly overhead, beside the Hall of Honor.

Stone markers carry the seals of each military branch. The group walked the grounds and gathered for photos as the day began.
